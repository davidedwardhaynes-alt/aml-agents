Singapore's two scam announcements this week aren't separate stories. They're the same strategy split into a detection layer and an enforcement layer.

The AI pilot with banks and law enforcement is about seeing the fund flow across institutions that no single bank can see alone. The Scams (Countermeasures) Bill, passed on 9 September, is about acting on what's seen — disclosure orders, account freezes, harsher penalties for mules and non-compliant platforms.

Neither works well without the other. Cross-bank detection without freeze powers just produces a faster alert that nobody can act on in time. Freeze powers without shared visibility catch the account, not the network moving the money through it.

The numbers explain the urgency: 90 scam reports a day, roughly SGD 2 million in daily losses, SGD 410.6 million lost in the first half of 2026 alone. MAS expects findings from the AI trial by year end, which is when the two pieces are meant to start operating together.
